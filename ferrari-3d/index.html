    Ferrari 458 Italia 2009 — 2015 Design / Pininfarina

    A mid-engined V8 from Maranello: 4.5 litres, no turbochargers, a seven-speed dual-clutch gearbox and bodywork shaped as much by the wind tunnel as by the pen. This is a study in how it moves.

    The last great Aspirated V8.

    02Overture

    Born at nine thousand revolutions.

    Nine thousand rpm. 127 horsepower per litre. A flat-plane crank that screams instead of rumbles. The 458 was engineered around a single sensation — the moment the needle stops climbing and the horizon starts arriving.

    03Design

    Sculpted by air.

    Front winglets that flex at speed to cut drag. A shape with no spoiler, because the body is the spoiler. Every surface earns its place in the wind tunnel.

    04Anatomy

    Out of the dark.

    Before the numbers, the object. One line of light travelling down the flank of a car built from separate parts.

    Every part, in its place.

    Clamshells open on their hinges, doors swing from the A-pillar, wheels slide off their hubs and the cockpit rises in layers. Each part leaves along the path it was fitted on.

    The power unit.

    Lifted straight out of the front bay, axles below. In this study model it is an envelope: the space a power unit occupies, not the engine itself.

    Back together.

    Reverse order, same paths. Powertrain and cockpit first, then wheels, doors and body. Every part returns to the exact position it left.

    Whole again.

    Engineering you will never see, so that everything you do see can be this simple.
